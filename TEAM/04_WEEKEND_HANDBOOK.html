What we are building, in plain words

The government measures inflation by checking prices every month. For air travel they use one number a month for the whole country. Its weight in the basket is 0.08.

Air tickets do not behave like that. The same seat can cost ₹6,000 today and ₹24,000 tomorrow. We measured exactly that this week.

APIx checks 6 routes at 5 booking windows, every day. That is 30 prices a day instead of one a month. Then it turns those prices into a single index number, the same way the government turns grocery prices into the CPI.

We are not building a flight-price scraper. We are building a measuring instrument that happens to use the web to read prices. That difference is the whole project, and it is what will separate us from AXIOM on Monday.

Words you will see everywhere

CELL
One route + one booking window + one day. Example: DEL–BOM, T+21, on 4 Sep. We have 30 cells a day.
T+21
A flight leaving 21 days from today. We check 5 of these: T+1, T+7, T+14, T+21, T+45.
BRONZE
The raw files exactly as the website gave them to us. Never edited, never deleted. Our evidence.
SILVER
The clean table. One row per flight price, pulled out of the Bronze files.
GOLD
The finished index numbers we publish. Built from Silver.
STATUS
Why a price is missing. Seven allowed values. A sold-out flight and a crashed scraper are different things and must never look the same.
COVERAGE
Out of the 30 prices we expected today, how many did we actually get? Published with every number.
THE GATE
Code that refuses to visit a website unless we checked today that the site allows it. It runs before the browser even opens.

The five-minute Q&A — learn these

QuestionWhoThe answer
Which index formula?BharatJevons — a geometric mean — for combining booking windows; Laspeyres-type fixed weights for combining routes. The same elementary form CPI construction uses.
Why median not average?BharatFare distributions are lopsided. On DEL–BOM, one route one date, we measured ₹6,090 up to ₹24,056. The average is about ₹8,900 — a price almost nobody paid. The median describes the ticket a normal traveller sees.
Where is the AI?MayankThere is none, deliberately. This measures, it does not predict. A model would make the number harder to audit, and a statistical office has to defend every figure it publishes.
The problem statement asks for CAPTCHA handling and IP rotation.MayankDeliberately absent. The same statement also requires robots.txt and terms compliance — both cannot be true at once. We built a permission-tiered system instead, and we report the sources that refuse as a finding about market observability.
How much of this is real?MayankThe MoSPI overlay is real official data. The compliance checks are real, run today. The collection is real — 30 cells a day from a permitted source. The 91-day history behind it is simulated from a documented model, labelled SIMULATED in the database, and drawn differently on the chart.
Six routes out of 1,100 city pairs?BharatAnswer per route, never in total. Five observations a day is about 150 a month, against the government's one national monthly figure. Adding a route is one line of config.
A festival surge and a broken parser both look like a spike.AyushThey arrive as different statuses. A surge is a run of OK rows and the median resists it. A broken parser is PARSE_FAIL — no fare, coverage drops, confidence downgrades, and the raw file is kept so we can re-parse the history later.
What if the website redesigns its page?MayankRows become PARSE_FAIL, coverage falls, the grade drops, and the series says so rather than quietly making a number up. Because the raw archive is immutable, fixing the parser recovers the history afterwards.
