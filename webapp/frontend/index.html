01

India publishes one airfare number a month. We publish thirty a day.

Every fare in the country reaches the Consumer Price Index as a single national figure at weight 0.08. Last year it fell 4.4% between June and July, and there was no way to ask which route moved, which week it happened, or whether it hit people who book early or people who book late. Both lines start at 100 on their own first day, so what you are comparing is movement and frequency, not rupee level.

05

A sold-out flight and a crashed collector look identical. We never record them the same way.

Both leave an empty cell. One is a fact about the market, the other is a fact about us. Write both down as blank and the index can no longer say how well it measured anything. Every observation carries a reason, from a list of seven the database will not let us add an eighth to.

06

We only collect where we are allowed to

Every source is checked before collection starts. We fetch the robots.txt, hash it, and store the file itself. A source not cleared today cannot be reached at all: the gate fires before the browser is even launched. There is no CAPTCHA handling and no IP rotation anywhere in this codebase, and the sources we could not clear are published below as a gap rather than hidden.

Take the numbers directly

A read API, so a statistical office or a central bank can pull the series into their own systems instead of scraping this page.

Two tiers. /public is open, rate limited, and needs no key. A published statistical series is public information, and it is what this dashboard itself reads. /v1 is the same data with a key attached, so bulk integration can be attributed, rate limited and revoked.

Keys are 256 bits of randomness, shown once at creation and stored only as a SHA-256 hash. A lost key gets rotated, not recovered. Scopes are enforced on the server: read:index, read:lineage, read:compliance.

08

Why this exists

A seat is not a bag of rice

Retail inflation is measured by collecting prices on a schedule. That works when the price of a thing is the price of a thing. Air travel enters the Consumer Price Index as one national figure, published once a month, at weight 0.08.

The same Delhi to Mumbai sector can cost ₹6,090 or ₹24,056 on the same day, depending on how far ahead you book, which day of the week you fly, and what else is happening that week. We measured that spread ourselves on 3 September. Over 90% of domestic tickets are sold online, which is where all of that movement lives, and none of it survives a monthly national average.

Thirty prices a day

APIx prices six representative city pairs at five booking distances, every day, at the same time. Quotes are cleaned, outliers are flagged inside their own cell, and the result is aggregated the way a CPI elementary aggregate is built: a Jevons geometric mean across booking windows, then a fixed-weight arithmetic mean across routes.

Every value we publish carries the coverage it was built from, a confidence grade, and a path back to the bytes it came from.

How the number is built

P(r,w,t) = median of the OK fares in that cell
R(r,w,t) = P(r,w,t) / P(r,w,base)
I(r,t)   = 100 × (R₁ × … × R_W)^(1/W)     Jevons
APIx(t)  = Σ ω_r × I(r,t),  Σ ω_r = 1     Laspeyres-type

Median, not average. Fare distributions lean hard to one side. The mean of that Delhi to Mumbai cell was about ₹8,900, a price almost nobody in it actually paid. The median was ₹7,675.

Geometric across windows. By that point they are ratios, and ratios multiply. A doubling and a halving cancel to exactly 100. A plain average would say 125.

Arithmetic across routes. Each route carries a real share of what travellers spend, and budget shares add. Weights are currently equal and flagged provisional, pending DGCA city-pair passenger data.

What is wrong with this, in our own words

Written down here so nobody has to find it.

  • Real collection started on 3 September. The longer history is generated from a documented model, labelled at every layer and drawn dashed. Nothing real is ever computed from it.
  • Route weights are equal and flagged provisional, pending DGCA city-pair passenger data.
  • The base is a single day in v0.1. It becomes a seven-day mean at v0.2, and the whole series gets recomputed.
  • Six routes out of roughly 1,100 city pairs. Adding a route is one line of configuration.
  • Five of eleven registered sources could not be verified. They timed out, which is not proof of a bot wall, and we record it as exactly that.
  • The daily series still carries a day-of-week cycle. Every window moves its departure date forward each day, so the basket rotates through the weekly fare cycle. The standard treatment is a seven-day moving average published alongside the raw series.
  • The brief asks for a 30-day backtest against DGCA monthly average fares. We do not hold that dataset. The hand-checked panel is a substitute, not an equivalent.
