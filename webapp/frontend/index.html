06

Compliance

Every source is checked before collection. The robots.txt is fetched, hashed and stored, and a source not cleared today cannot be reached — the gate fires before the browser is launched. There is no CAPTCHA handling and no IP rotation in this codebase.

07

Consuming the index

A read API so a statistical office or a central bank can take the series directly instead of scraping a dashboard.

Two tiers. /public is open and rate limited — a published statistical series is public information, and it is what this dashboard reads. /v1 is the documented integration surface and takes a key, so bulk access is attributable, rate-limitable and revocable.

Authentication

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer apix_live_..." \
     https://apix.example.gov.in/v1/apix/latest

curl -H "X-API-Key: apix_live_..." \
     https://apix.example.gov.in/v1/lead-time

Keys are 256 bits of randomness, shown once at creation and stored only as a SHA-256 hash. A lost key is rotated, not recovered. Scopes are enforced server-side: read:index, read:lineage, read:compliance.

08

The problem, and what this does about it

One number a month

Retail inflation is measured by collecting prices on a schedule. Air travel enters the Consumer Price Index as a single national figure, published monthly, at weight 0.08.

An airline seat does not behave like a bag of rice. The same sector can move several hundred percent inside a day depending on booking window, day of week, demand surges and fuel-linked surcharges. Over 90% of domestic tickets are now sold online, where that movement happens — and none of it reaches a monthly national average.

Thirty prices a day

APIx prices six representative city pairs at five advance-purchase windows, every day, at a fixed observation slot. Those quotes are cleaned, outliers are flagged inside their own cell, and the result is aggregated the way a CPI elementary aggregate is built: a Jevons geometric mean across booking windows, then a fixed-weight arithmetic mean across routes.

Every published value carries the coverage it was built from, a confidence grade, and a path back to the bytes it came from.

How the number is built

P(r,w,t) = median of the OK fares in that cell
R(r,w,t) = P(r,w,t) / P(r,w,base)
I(r,t)   = 100 × (R₁ × … × R_W)^(1/W)     Jevons
APIx(t)  = Σ ω_r × I(r,t),  Σ ω_r = 1     Laspeyres-type

Median, not average — fare distributions are lopsided, and an average reports a price almost nobody paid.

Geometric across windows — they are ratios by that point, and ratios multiply. A doubling and a halving cancel to exactly 100; a plain average would say 125.

Arithmetic across routes — each route carries a real share of what travellers spend, and budget shares add. Weights are equal and flagged provisional pending DGCA city-pair passenger data.

Stated limitations

Recorded here rather than left to be found.

  • Real collection is recent; the longer history is generated from a documented model, labelled at every layer and drawn dashed. Nothing real is computed from it.
  • Route weights are equal and flagged provisional, pending DGCA city-pair passenger data.
  • Single-day base in v0.1; a seven-day mean at v0.2, with the series recomputed.
  • Six routes of roughly 1,100 city pairs. Adding a route is one line of configuration.
  • Five of eleven registered sources could not be verified — read timeouts, which is not proof of a bot wall and is recorded as exactly that.
  • The daily series carries a day-of-week cycle that has not been removed: every window shifts its departure date forward daily, so the basket rotates through the weekly fare cycle. The standard treatment is a seven-day moving average alongside the raw series.
  • The brief asks for a 30-day backtest against DGCA monthly average fares. That dataset is not held, and the hand-checked panel is a substitute rather than an equivalent.
